Last Week's Sermon

▶  Watch the sermon on YouTube

With a new term in the air, Mark Fletcher turned to Ecclesiastes and its old question: what do we gain from all our toil? Work is good — we were made for it, and satisfaction in it is God's gift. But our society makes an idol of work, letting our jobs define who we are — and like every idol, it lets us down. The answer is rhythm: work and rest in their seasons, and an identity that rests not in what we do but in the God who set eternity in our hearts.

FACCV recognition in the Canton of Vaud

On Wednesday 2nd September at Christchurch Lausanne we gathered with Christians from across Vaud and beyond to celebrate the recognition of Federation of Anglican and Catholic-Christian Churches in the Canton of Vaud. It was a joyful service recognising a historic event, the culmination of 10 years of hard work. We were joined by Bishop Robert Innes, Bishop of Europe, and Bishop Frank, Bishop of the Old Catholic Church as well as representatives of the Canton of Vaud.

Revd Mark Fletcher and members of our congregation represented St Peter's. An interview with FACCV appeared this week in Réformés.

Scripture Union: inauguration and blessing at Le Clos des Pierres

Over the weekend of 28–30 August, the Ligue pour la lecture de la Bible — the Swiss branch of Scripture Union — invited the local churches, communes and companies to celebrate the renovation of Le Clos des Pierres, above Rougemont, and to thank them for their help with the project. Hundreds of people came to see a former farmhouse, dating from 1773, transformed into a modern youth centre, with accommodation for up to 49, a full professional dining room, and room for day events of up to 100.

The weekend ended on the Sunday with a joint celebration and service for over 150 people. Loulou the lion entertained young and old, the local alphorn ensemble played throughout the morning, and everyone enjoyed the saucisses de veau and local cheeses over lunch. The prayer was that the Gospel would be shared with generations of young people, with fun and fellowship. The centre is also available for local celebrations, such as weddings.

Why did a British soldier write to his fiancée from Château-d'Œx in 1916?

In August, Peter Murray and his wife Carol came to Château-d'Œx from Essex, bringing with them fourteen of Peter's grandfather's postcards from Switzerland — eleven of them posted from this village more than a century ago. The family still keeps around ten more. Private Edward Ford of the East Surrey Regiment had been taken prisoner, and his family's account is that he was gassed at Ypres. In 1916 Switzerland agreed to receive sick and wounded prisoners of war from the German camps, and Edward was in Château-d'Œx within days of the first arrivals that June, when some 450 British internees were welcomed into the village and a thanksgiving service was held in the English church.

Edward was lodged at the Grand Hôtel Beau Séjour, and his postcards to Lily, his fiancée in London, record the small mercies of that summer: the local paper he asked Lily to send him (the Willesden Chronicle), a snowfall in June, a bicycle ride to Rougemont, cigarettes when they could be had — and on Sunday 3 September 1916, almost exactly 110 years ago this week, a few words dashed off before the morning service: “just going to Church.”

The cards stayed in the family for over a hundred years. Now Peter and Carol have brought them home: they have given them to St Peter's, back in the village where Edward wrote them.

There is much more to tell. We are putting together a longer article on Edward Ford and the story his postcards tell, which will be published on our website shortly.

St Peter's Life Group

The St Peter’s Lifegroup resumes its weekly gatherings on Zoom on Monday 7th September at 19.00 Swiss time.

We are a small group of Jesus followers trying to make sense of the Bible and life.

Each week we seek to discover the wisdom in Scripture that can shape us into a community that trusts God, lives justly and loves both God and neighbour.

We are planning a more varied programme, so that in any month we might meditate on a Psalm, study a biblical theme, examine a character or one of the commandments. We are beginning on Monday 7th with the topic of “encouragement”. We would love you to join us.
